Skyhoard

Two dwarf crews. Two airships. One floating island.

Skyhoard is a first-person multiplayer game about crews of dwarves who fly forgeships: armoured airships of timber and iron. Take the wheel, man the cannon, and shoot out the other ship's engines and lift crystals. Plates crack and fall away, bare timber breaches and burns, and somebody has to grab the sand.

  • Fly the forgeship

    Steer from the wheel on the quarterdeck. Twenty-four metres of timber and iron over three decks, with a forge in her belly.

  • Break the other ship

    Cannon fire cracks hull plates, blows them off and holes the timber behind. Engines and lift crystals can be shot out.

  • Fight the fire

    Bare timber burns, and a fire keeps spreading until someone puts it out with sand.

  • Grapple and climb

    A rope that wraps round edges. Climb cliffs and ship sides, and rivet new plates on while hanging off the hull.

  • Mine, fight and ram

    Dig ore on the island, fight its golems, or ram the other ship at full speed.

  • Play with friends

    Host a match and share a join code. Nobody has to open a port.

How we build

Built by one developer and Claude.

Skyhoard's code, its 3D ship, its build scripts and its tests are all written by Claude Code, working from the command line. The Unity editor has never been opened by hand. The developer sets the direction, plays every build and makes the calls.

  1. Direct

    The developer describes the feature, the feel or the fix, and reviews pictures of the result.

  2. Write

    Claude Code writes the C# game code, the scripts that assemble each scene and model the ship, and Python that synthesizes most of the sound effects.

  3. Build

    One command builds the scene, builds the game and runs the tests.

  4. Test

    The game plays itself: a hidden walk-through, two hidden copies playing each other online, and benchmark runs.

  5. Play

    A playtest build goes out, and whatever feels wrong goes back to the first step.

The studio

One developer. Claude as the crew.

Sarensong Studios was founded in 2026 by a solo developer. Design, direction, music and every final decision come from one desk, and the engineering is done hand in hand with Claude.

Working this way lets one person build a networked 3D game with its own ship pipeline, test suite and benchmarks, work that would normally need a team.
